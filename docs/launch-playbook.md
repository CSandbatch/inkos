# InkOS launch playbook

## Positioning

InkOS is a local agent production OS for fiction: plan, draft, audit, and close a novel through an inspectable workflow you control.

The primary audience is technically adventurous authors. Lead with creative outcomes and privacy; use the event store, story graph, and durable DAG as evidence. Never claim that a closure gate guarantees literary quality or commercial success.

## Demonstration story

Use **A Clock Without Hands**, the bundled mystery fixture:

1. Show the manuscript establishing that a clock stopped at 11:43.
2. Show a witness claiming to hear it strike midnight.
3. Open the graph edge connecting the witness to the impossible evidence.
4. Show the audit block and the canon-changing repair proposal.
5. Reject an unsupported retcon, revise the testimony, and rerun the audit.
6. Show the obligation move from open to resolved and the closure report change.

This sequence demonstrates continuity, mystery fairness, author authority, attributable revisions, and publication gates in under one minute.

## Launch sequence

- **Public alpha:** accurate README, security policy, roadmap, project topics, Discussions, and local install path.
- **Build in public:** one weekly artifact explaining a specific author problem and its evidence-backed repair.
- **Preview:** seeded demo, npm canary, clean-install proof, and contributor documentation.
- **v1 promotion:** complete vertical slice, narrated walkthrough, closure benchmark, and coordinated author-community posts.
- **First month:** respond to launch defects within 48 hours and publish weekly onboarding improvements.

## Channel adaptation

- Mystery communities: focus on retroactive clue prevention and reader solvability.
- General writing communities: focus on continuity, revision history, and local privacy.
- Writing-tool communities: focus on import/export boundaries and author control.
- Developer communities: focus on SQLite transactions, typed DAG nodes, idempotency, and capability separation.

Posts must teach through the fixture rather than repeat a generic product announcement. Follow each community's self-promotion rules and disclose the maintainer relationship.

## Metrics

The primary activation metric is a completed seeded workflow. Supporting metrics are documentation-to-install clicks, clean-install success, first local project, seven-day return, documentation searches with no result, release adoption, and issue response time. Stars and page views are context, not product success.

No manuscript content, project identity, prompt, path, credential, or model response may be collected to calculate these metrics.
